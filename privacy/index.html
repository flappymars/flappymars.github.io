Flappy Mars Privacy Policy

Last updated: 12 November 2026

Who we are

Flappy Mars is a game made by an individual developer in Italy, who publishes it under the name Flappy Mars. For anything about your privacy, write to flappymars@gmail.com.

This page describes the version of Flappy Mars on Google Play.

In short

  • The game shows ads from Google (Google AdMob), and you can buy REMOVE ADS once to remove them. It has no account of its own. For statistics it uses only Google's tools, among them Google Analytics for Firebase, which counts how the game is played (see «Statistics» below).
  • Google explains how it uses your data in its ads here: business.safety.google/privacy
  • The game itself sends nothing to us.
  • The game keeps on your device your best score in each version of the game, the skin you chose, how many times you have started the game (counted up to 3), the date of the last time it asked for the rating window and how many times it has asked for it, the scores that could not yet be sent to the leaderboards, how long you have flown since the last ad and whether an ad is due, whether you bought REMOVE ADS, and whether the statistics are on or off for you. Google's consent tool, which is inside the game, also keeps on your device the choices you make about ads.
  • The game on Google Play uses seven Google services: the rating window, the updates, the leaderboards, the ads, the consent window, the payment of REMOVE ADS and the statistics of Google Analytics for Firebase. Through them Google receives: the stars and the text you write in the rating window; data about your device and the version of the game, to check for updates; the analytics and diagnostics data of its Play Games library, which is inside the game; if you have a Google Play Games profile, your score at the end of every game and when the game goes to the background during a game, linked to your gamer name and your picture; for the ads, your device's advertising ID and app set ID, your IP address, how you use the game and how it performs (see «Ads» below); for the statistics, only if you allow them where Google's consent window appears, what happens in your games, an identifier of your installation of the game, your advertising ID and your approximate location (see «Statistics» below); and, if you buy REMOVE ADS, your purchase, which the statistics also count.
  • Google shows us your reviews, statistics about the game and its players, including those of Google Analytics for Firebase, and the orders of REMOVE ADS.

What the game keeps on your device

The game keeps on your device:

  • your best score in each version of the game;
  • the date and time of the last time it asked Google Play for the rating window, and how many times it has asked for it (see below), so that it does not ask again before 15 days have passed, nor more than 4 times;
  • how many times you have started the game, counted only up to 3 (coming back to it from the background does not count), so that it does not ask for the rating window before the third time (see below);
  • the skin you chose to fly with, and which of the skins you won still show the word NEW;
  • the best score of each version that could not be sent to the leaderboards, for example because you were not signed in to Google Play Games, so that it can be sent the next time you sign in (see «Without a profile» below);
  • how many seconds you have flown since the last full-screen ad or the last ad video, so that a full-screen ad comes only after 5 minutes of flight;
  • whether a full-screen ad is due but put off to the end of your next game (the game keeps these two items also if you say no in Google's consent window: they never leave your device, and they only serve to show you full-screen ads less often);
  • whether you bought REMOVE ADS, as Google Play says (see «Buying REMOVE ADS» below);
  • whether the statistics of Google Analytics are on or off for you, as your last answer in Google's consent window decided, so that the game knows it also when it cannot reach Google.

They are kept in the game's own folder, which other apps cannot read. The game does not send these items anywhere, except the scores waiting for the leaderboards (see below); what the leaderboards receive, and the best scores they can give back, are explained below. The game itself writes nothing else about you on your device. Google's ads tool, which is inside the game, may also store and read information on your device, as explained in «Ads» below. Google's consent tool, which is inside the game, also keeps on your device the choices you make in its consent window. These items, and your choices about ads, are deleted when you uninstall the game or clear its data in the settings of your device. If you bought REMOVE ADS, the game learns it again from Google Play when you reinstall it with the same Google account.

The rating window

After a GAME OVER with a new record of at least 11 points, the game asks Google Play to show its rating window: the first time only from the third time you start the game, then only if it has not asked in the last 15 days, and at most 4 times in all. Google decides whether to show it, and it has its own limits on how often.

The window belongs to Google Play, not to the game. The stars and the text you write go to Google Play: the game never sees them, and Google does not even tell the game whether you rated or whether the window appeared. Google says this data is encrypted.

Your rating becomes a public review on Google Play, linked to your Google account. You can change or delete it at any time from the game's page in the Google Play Store app (how to do it). If you rate the game as a tester of a closed test, Google does not publish the rating: it shares it privately with us. To take part in a closed test, you join our Google Group with your Google account, and we can see the list of the group's members. We keep the group while we use it to test new versions of the game, and we delete it when we stop, or after 12 months without a test. You can leave the group at any time from Google Groups (how to do it).

Google Play shows us each review with your user name and its date, and some reviews also with details about the device and the game, such as the maker, the screen size, the system, the version of the game and the language. We can reply to reviews.

Updates

When you open the game, it asks the Google Play Store app on your device whether a newer version of the game is available. To answer, Google Play uses data about your device, the version of the game and the list of its installed parts. Google says this data is encrypted, is not passed to third parties, and is deleted after a fixed period. The game uses only the answer, that is whether there is a newer version and its number. When that version must be installed to keep playing (see below), the game keeps its number on your device, and nothing else, until it is installed or Google Play no longer offers it: so the game still knows that the update is needed if Google Play cannot answer the next time.

When we decide that a new version must be installed to keep playing, Google Play's update screen opens over the game; if you accept, Google Play installs the update and starts the game again. If you close that screen, the game asks you to update, and its UPDATE button asks Google Play again.

The leaderboards

The button with the podium and the word RANKING on the first screen open the Flappy Mars leaderboards. They are run by Google Play Games, Google's games service. There is one leaderboard for each version of the game, open to all players, with the best scores of today, of this week and of all time.

When you open the game, Google Play Games signs you in by itself, if you have a Play Games profile on your device. If you do not have a Play Games profile, nothing is asked when you open the game: Google asks you whether you want to create one only when you tap the button with the podium or the word RANKING. Once you are signed in, Google gives the game access to your Play Games identity: your gamer name and your picture, and also, among other things, a Play Games player ID and your Play Games level. At the end of every game (at GAME OVER, or when a game is left before its end, for example with the Back key), and also when the game goes to the background during a game (for example when you press the Home button, open another app or switch off the screen), the game sends your score to Google, for the leaderboard of the version you played. When you open the game, and after you sign in from one of those buttons, the game also asks Google Play Games for your scores in the all-time leaderboards: if one is higher than the best score of its version on your device, the game keeps it as your best score in that version, so that your record is not lost when you change device. The game uses your identity only for the leaderboards, and it sends none of it to us.

Google says that its Play Games library, which is inside the game, also collects analytics and diagnostics data by itself, to make its libraries more stable and to improve its products. Google says this data travels encrypted (HTTPS). Google handles all of this under its own privacy policy, and explains how it uses data from apps that use its services. Google also checks the scores, and hides by itself those that look tampered with.

Who sees your score. Google says that its public leaderboards show only the players who have chosen to share their gameplay activity publicly, and that if you make your Play Games profile public you can compare your scores with other players; it does not say clearly whether a private profile keeps your score off the leaderboard. New Play Games profiles start private. Even a private profile shows other players your gamer name, your picture, how many followers you have and how many people you follow; the followers you approve also see your whole profile, for example your achievements and your gamer stats. On a leaderboard, tapping a player opens their Play Games profile. You can make your profile public or private at any time in its settings (how to do it). These choices are made in your Play Games profile, not in the game.

Without a profile. You do not need a Play Games profile: if you say no, or do not have one, you play the whole game, only without the leaderboards. When the game cannot send a score, for example because you are not signed in, it keeps on your device the best score of each version not sent. If you sign in later, for example by creating a profile from the button with the podium or the word RANKING, the game sends those scores to Google for the leaderboards, and then forgets them. If you never sign in, they never leave your device.

Deleting. You can delete the Play Games data of Flappy Mars, or your whole Play Games profile: in the Google Play Store app, tap your profile picture, then Settings, General, Play Games profile, Privacy & Settings; or on the web, at play.google.com/games/profile (how to do it). Google warns that deleting your whole profile can also delete your scores on leaderboards. If Web & App Activity is on in your Google account, Google also saves your game activity there: you can see it and delete it in My Google Activity.

Ads

Unless you buy REMOVE ADS, the game shows ads from Google AdMob, Google's ads service, but never while you fly.

Google's ads tool, which is inside the game, sends Google: your device's advertising ID, its app set ID, an identifier that the tool creates for the game and changes over time and, if there are any, identifiers of the accounts signed in on your device; your IP address, from which Google can estimate your approximate location; how you use the game, for example when you open it, your taps and the ads you watch; and how the game and the tool perform, for example loading time, freezes and battery use. Google says these data travel encrypted. Google and its ad partners use these data to show ads and measure them, to know how the game and the ads perform, and to prevent fraud. To do so, they may store and read information on your device, such as identifiers.

Google receives these data as an independent controller: it decides itself how to use them, under its own privacy policy. For people in the European Economic Area or Switzerland, this is Google Ireland Limited. The ads can come from Google or from other ad companies that work with Google (its ad partners). Where the consent window appears, its «Ad partners» page lists them by name. Google lists the ad partners most often used here: support.google.com/admob/answer/9012903

Where Google says it is needed, for example in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, the game shows Google's consent window the first time you open it with an internet connection. There you choose whether Google and its ad partners may store and read information on your device and show you personalised ads. Under «Manage options» you can choose purpose by purpose, see the list of ad partners, and object to the uses that rely on legitimate interest. If you say no, Google can still show limited ads, which are not personalised. To deliver them, Google still uses data such as your IP address. To fight fraud, Google may also store and read information on your device for these ads. If you allow Google to store information on your device but say no to personalised ads, the ads are not personalised, but they still use your advertising ID to limit how often you see the same ad and to count the ads.

Where the consent window does not appear, the ads can be personalised. Wherever you live, you can reset or delete your device's advertising ID in the settings of your device, and choose how Google shows you ads in your Google ad settings (how to do it).

You can change or withdraw your choice at any time, as easily as you made it, with the PRIVACY CHOICES button on the CREDITS page, which is there wherever Google says it is needed.

Google says that it anonymizes advertising data in its server logs, removing part of the IP address after 9 months.

Buying REMOVE ADS

REMOVE ADS is a purchase you make once, through Google Play, and it removes the ads from the game. The game asks Google Play whether you bought it, and confirms your purchase to Google Play with a code that Google Play gives for that purchase. On your device it keeps only whether you bought it. The game never sees your payment details, and it sends nothing about your purchase to us.

Google Play handles the payment under the Google Payments privacy notice when you pay with your Google Payments account. Google says that it collects, among other things, the names and email addresses of the seller and of the buyer and the date, time and amount of the purchase, and that it shares personal data with the developer you buy from.

In its Play Console, Google Play shows us each order, with the item, its price, taxes and fees and the order history, and lets us find an order by its number or by the buyer's full email address. Google Play may also give us your name and email address. We use these data only to manage your purchase. Google decides how long the orders stay in Play Console.

Statistics

The game uses Google Analytics for Firebase, Google's statistics tool, to learn how the game is played, so that we can make it better. The game itself sends it only four kinds of events: when a game starts, with the version you play (EASY, NORMAL or HARD); when you pass a level, with its name, the round and the version; when you lose a life, with the level, the rock you hit, the round and the version; and when a game ends, with the score, how many levels you reached, the level where it ended and the version.

With these events, and also by itself, Google Analytics collects: an identifier of your installation of the game, your device's advertising ID, your IP address, which it uses to estimate your approximate location, when and for how long you use the game, the screens you see, and, if you buy REMOVE ADS, your purchase with the name and the price of the product. The game does not give Google Analytics your name, your Play Games profile or any identifier of its own.

Where Google's consent window appears, Google Analytics collects these data only after you agree there both to the storing and reading of information on your device and to Google among the ad partners. Before you answer, if you say no, or if you later withdraw your consent with the PRIVACY CHOICES button, the game sends Google Analytics nothing. Where the consent window does not appear, Google Analytics collects these data from everyone who plays the version on Google Play.

Google says these data travel encrypted. Google processes these data for us, to give us the statistics, and we have not allowed Google to use them to improve its own products and services. What we share with Google AdMob is explained just below.

We have linked these statistics to our Google AdMob account, so Google Analytics also shares these data with Google AdMob, including your purchase of REMOVE ADS. Google AdMob receives them as an independent controller, as for the ads (see «Ads» above), and uses them to show us data about the players next to the data about the ads, to improve its features and to help apps earn more from ads.

Google Analytics keeps the details of these events for 2 months, and the data linked to your installation of the game for 14 months, counted again from the last time you play.

When you write to us

The words CONTACT ME on the first screen open your device's email app with a message already addressed to us. At the bottom it has a few lines that help us answer: the name and version of the game, the model of your device and the version of its system. Nothing is sent until you press send yourself, and you can delete those lines before sending.

If you write to us, we receive your email address and what you write. We use them only to answer you, and we do not share them with anyone. Our mailbox is a Gmail mailbox, so the messages are kept by Google. We delete a message, together with our replies, no later than 12 months after our last reply, or sooner if you ask us to.

This page

The PRIVACY POLICY button on the CREDITS page of the game opens this page in your browser. Where Google says it is needed, next to it there is a PRIVACY CHOICES button, which opens Google's consent window again, so that you can change your choices. The page is hosted by GitHub (GitHub Pages). When you open it, GitHub records your IP address for security purposes (GitHub Privacy Statement). The game itself sends nothing: it is your browser that opens the page.

Google Play

If you download the game from Google Play, Google handles your data under its own privacy policy and the Google Play Terms of Service. In its Play Console, Google shows us statistics on the game, for example how many people install it. From people who chose to share usage and diagnostics data with Google, it also shows us reports of when the game crashes or freezes, with the model of the device and the version of its system. For players signed in to Play Games, it shows us statistics on how they play, with approximate country, age and gender. We use these statistics only to improve the game.

Children

The game does not ask your age, and it handles the data of children as it handles everyone's, as this page explains. It asks Google only for ads suitable for all ages (rating G). For a child under 13 (or the age set in their country) with a Google account managed by a parent with Family Link, Google asks a parent to approve before the child can create a Play Games profile.

In the European Union, a young person can consent alone to the ads only from the age set by their country, between 13 and 16 (in Italy, 14). Below that age, a parent must make the choices in the consent window.

Legal basis

We use your data on three legal bases of the GDPR. To give you the game you downloaded and its features (keeping your best score on your device, checking for updates and, if you have a Play Games profile, sending your scores to the leaderboards and taking your best scores back from them, and, if you buy REMOVE ADS, checking your purchase with Google Play and remembering it on your device), because it is necessary to give you the game and what you bought (Article 6(1)(b)). To remember on your device when we last asked you to rate the game, how many times we asked and how many times you started the game, how long you have flown since the last ad and whether an ad is due, to read the statistics, crash reports and reviews that Google shows us, to answer your emails and to keep the list of the closed-test group, because we have a legitimate interest in asking for your opinion without asking too often, in not showing you ads too often, in fixing and improving the game, in answering you and in testing new versions before they reach everyone (Article 6(1)(f)). Where Google's consent window appears, the ads use your data only as you chose there: on your consent (Article 6(1)(a)) to store and read information on your device and to show personalised ads; for some other purposes, such as measuring the ads, Google relies on its own legitimate interest, which you can refuse in the window under «Manage options». Where Google's consent window appears, the statistics too use your data only on your consent (Article 6(1)(a)), the one you give there: if you say no, or withdraw it, the game sends Google Analytics nothing (see «Statistics» above). Where Google's consent window does not appear, the ads and the statistics use your data on our legitimate interest in offering the game for free, paid by the ads, and in learning how it is played so that we can improve it (Article 6(1)(f)). You can ask us how we weighed this interest against yours.

Your right to object. When we use your data because of our legitimate interest, you can object at any time, for reasons related to your situation, by writing to flappymars@gmail.com. Where Google's consent window does not appear, you can also object at any time to the use of your data for the ads and the statistics: for personalised ads, delete or reset the advertising ID in the settings of your device (see «Ads» above); for everything else, write to flappymars@gmail.com.

Your right to withdraw consent. You can withdraw your consent to the ads at any time, as easily as you gave it, with the PRIVACY CHOICES button on the CREDITS page (see «Ads» above).

Where your data goes

Google and GitHub belong to groups based in the United States, and they may handle data in the United States or in other countries outside the European Union, for example the emails in our Gmail mailbox, the record of visits to this page, and the data of the ads, of REMOVE ADS and of the statistics. Both say that they follow the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework, which the European Commission recognised in July 2023 as giving adequate protection, and that they also use, where needed, the standard contractual clauses of the European Commission (Google: data transfer frameworks; GitHub: GitHub Privacy Statement).

Your rights

Wherever you live, you have the rights given by the European data protection law (GDPR), in the cases it provides: you can ask us to see the data we have about you, to correct it or delete it, to limit how we use it, to object to its use, and to receive it in a form you can reuse. Write to flappymars@gmail.com. Apart from the list of the closed-test group, the only data about you that we keep ourselves are the messages you send us. The data of the rating window, of the updates, of Play Games, of the ads, of REMOVE ADS and of the statistics are kept by Google, and your choices about ads are kept on your device: you can manage them with the tools described above, or in your Google account.

You can also complain to a data protection authority, in the country where you live or work, or where you think the law was broken. In Italy it is the Garante per la protezione dei dati personali. The list of the European authorities is on the website of the European Data Protection Board.

Changes

If a new version of the game changes what happens to your data, for example with new Google Play features or with levels to buy, we will change this page before that version comes out, and the date at the top will say when.
