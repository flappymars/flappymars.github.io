Flappy Mars Privacy Policy

Last updated: 27 October 2026

Who we are

Flappy Mars is a game made by an individual developer in Italy, who publishes it under the name Flappy Mars. For anything about your privacy, write to flappymars@gmail.com.

This page describes the version of Flappy Mars on Google Play.

In short

  • The game has no advertising, nothing to buy and no account of its own. It contains no advertising tool and no statistics tool of our own: the only statistics come from Google, as explained below.
  • The game itself sends nothing to us.
  • The game keeps on your device your best score in each version of the game and the date of the last time it asked for the rating window.
  • The game on Google Play uses three Google services: the rating window, the updates and the leaderboards. Through them Google receives: the stars and the text you write in the rating window; data about your device and the version of the game, to check for updates; the analytics and diagnostics data of its Play Games library, which is inside the game; and, if you have a Google Play Games profile, your score at the end of every game and when the game goes to the background during a game, linked to your gamer name and your picture.
  • Google shows us your reviews, and statistics about the game and its players.

What the game keeps on your device

The game keeps on your device:

  • your best score in each version of the game;
  • the date and time of the last time it asked Google Play for the rating window (see below), so that it does not ask again before 15 days have passed.

They are kept in the game's own folder, which other apps cannot read. The game does not send these two items anywhere; what the leaderboards receive, and the best scores they can give back, are explained below. The game itself writes nothing else about you on your device. These two items are deleted when you uninstall the game or clear its data in the settings of your device.

The rating window

After a GAME OVER with a new record of at least 11 points, the game asks Google Play to show its rating window, if it has not asked in the last 15 days. Google decides whether to show it, and it has its own limits on how often.

The window belongs to Google Play, not to the game. The stars and the text you write go to Google Play: the game never sees them, and Google does not even tell the game whether you rated or whether the window appeared. Google says this data is encrypted.

Your rating becomes a public review on Google Play, linked to your Google account. You can change or delete it at any time from the game's page in the Google Play Store app (how to do it). If you rate the game as a tester of a closed test, Google does not publish the rating: it shares it privately with us. To take part in a closed test, you join our Google Group with your Google account, and we can see the list of the group's members. We keep the group while we use it to test new versions of the game, and we delete it when we stop, or after 12 months without a test. You can leave the group at any time from Google Groups (how to do it).

Google Play shows us each review with your user name and its date, and some reviews also with details about the device and the game, such as the maker, the screen size, the system, the version of the game and the language. We can reply to reviews.

Updates

When you open the game, it asks the Google Play Store app on your device whether a newer version of the game is available. To answer, Google Play uses data about your device, the version of the game and the list of its installed parts. Google says this data is encrypted, is not passed to third parties, and is deleted after a fixed period. The game uses only the answer, that is whether there is a newer version and its number. When that version must be installed to keep playing (see below), the game keeps its number on your device, and nothing else, until it is installed or Google Play no longer offers it: so the game still knows that the update is needed if Google Play cannot answer the next time.

When we decide that a new version must be installed to keep playing, Google Play's update screen opens over the game; if you accept, Google Play installs the update and starts the game again. If you close that screen, the game asks you to update, and its UPDATE button asks Google Play again.

The leaderboards

The button with the podium and the word RANKING on the first screen open the Flappy Mars leaderboards. They are run by Google Play Games, Google's games service. There is one leaderboard for each version of the game, open to all players, with the best scores of today, of this week and of all time.

When you open the game, Google Play Games signs you in by itself, if you have a Play Games profile on your device. If you do not have a Play Games profile, nothing is asked when you open the game: Google asks you whether you want to create one only when you tap the button with the podium or the word RANKING. Once you are signed in, Google gives the game access to your Play Games identity: your gamer name and your picture, and also, among other things, a Play Games player ID and your Play Games level. At the end of every game (at GAME OVER, or when a game is left before its end, for example with the Back key), and also when the game goes to the background during a game (for example when you press the Home button, open another app or switch off the screen), the game sends your score to Google, for the leaderboard of the version you played. When you open the game, and after you sign in from one of those buttons, the game also asks Google Play Games for your scores in the all-time leaderboards: if one is higher than the best score of its version on your device, the game keeps it as your best score in that version, so that your record is not lost when you change device. The game uses your identity only for the leaderboards, and it sends none of it to us.

Google says that its Play Games library, which is inside the game, also collects analytics and diagnostics data by itself, to make its libraries more stable and to improve its products. Google says this data travels encrypted (HTTPS). Google handles all of this under its own privacy policy, and explains how it uses data from apps that use its services. Google also checks the scores, and hides by itself those that look tampered with.

Who sees your score. Google says that its public leaderboards show only the players who have chosen to share their gameplay activity publicly, and that if you make your Play Games profile public you can compare your scores with other players; it does not say clearly whether a private profile keeps your score off the leaderboard. New Play Games profiles start private. Even a private profile shows other players your gamer name, your picture, how many followers you have and how many people you follow; the followers you approve also see your whole profile, for example your achievements and your gamer stats. On a leaderboard, tapping a player opens their Play Games profile. You can make your profile public or private at any time in its settings (how to do it). These choices are made in your Play Games profile, not in the game.

Without a profile. You do not need a Play Games profile: if you say no, or do not have one, you play the whole game, only without the leaderboards, and the game sends no score.

Deleting. You can delete the Play Games data of Flappy Mars, or your whole Play Games profile: in the Google Play Store app, tap your profile picture, then Settings, General, Play Games profile, Privacy & Settings; or on the web, at play.google.com/games/profile (how to do it). Google warns that deleting your whole profile can also delete your scores on leaderboards. If Web & App Activity is on in your Google account, Google also saves your game activity there: you can see it and delete it in My Google Activity.

When you write to us

The words CONTACT ME on the first screen open your device's email app with a message already addressed to us. At the bottom it has a few lines that help us answer: the name and version of the game, the model of your device and the version of its system. Nothing is sent until you press send yourself, and you can delete those lines before sending.

If you write to us, we receive your email address and what you write. We use them only to answer you, and we do not share them with anyone. Our mailbox is a Gmail mailbox, so the messages are kept by Google. We delete a message, together with our replies, no later than 12 months after our last reply, or sooner if you ask us to.

This page

The PRIVACY POLICY button on the CREDITS page of the game opens this page in your browser. The page is hosted by GitHub (GitHub Pages). When you open it, GitHub records your IP address for security purposes (GitHub Privacy Statement). The game itself sends nothing: it is your browser that opens the page.

Google Play

If you download the game from Google Play, Google handles your data under its own privacy policy and the Google Play Terms of Service. In its Play Console, Google shows us statistics on the game, for example how many people install it. From people who chose to share usage and diagnostics data with Google, it also shows us reports of when the game crashes or freezes, with the model of the device and the version of its system. For players signed in to Play Games, it shows us statistics on how they play, with approximate country, age and gender. We use these statistics only to improve the game.

Children

The game does not ask your age, and it handles the data of children as it handles everyone's, as this page explains. For a child under 13 (or the age set in their country) with a Google account managed by a parent with Family Link, Google asks a parent to approve before the child can create a Play Games profile.

Legal basis

We use your data on two legal bases of the GDPR. To give you the game you downloaded and its features (keeping your best score on your device, checking for updates and, if you have a Play Games profile, sending your scores to the leaderboards and taking your best scores back from them), because it is necessary to give you the game (Article 6(1)(b)). To remember on your device when we last asked you to rate the game, to read the statistics, crash reports and reviews that Google shows us, to answer your emails and to keep the list of the closed-test group, because we have a legitimate interest in asking for your opinion without asking too often, in fixing and improving the game, in answering you and in testing new versions before they reach everyone (Article 6(1)(f)). You can ask us how we weighed this interest against yours.

Your right to object. When we use your data because of our legitimate interest, you can object at any time, for reasons related to your situation, by writing to flappymars@gmail.com.

Where your data goes

Google and GitHub belong to groups based in the United States, and they may handle data in the United States or in other countries outside the European Union, for example the emails in our Gmail mailbox and the record of visits to this page. Both say that they follow the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework, which the European Commission recognised in July 2023 as giving adequate protection, and that they also use, where needed, the standard contractual clauses of the European Commission (Google: data transfer frameworks; GitHub: GitHub Privacy Statement).

Your rights

Wherever you live, you have the rights given by the European data protection law (GDPR), in the cases it provides: you can ask us to see the data we have about you, to correct it or delete it, to limit how we use it, to object to its use, and to receive it in a form you can reuse. Write to flappymars@gmail.com. Apart from the list of the closed-test group, the only data about you that we keep ourselves are the messages you send us. The data of the rating window, of the updates and of Play Games are kept by Google: you can manage them with the tools described above, or in your Google account.

You can also complain to a data protection authority, in the country where you live or work, or where you think the law was broken. In Italy it is the Garante per la protezione dei dati personali. The list of the European authorities is on the website of the European Data Protection Board.

Changes

If a new version of the game changes what happens to your data, for example with new Google Play features or with levels to buy, we will change this page before that version comes out, and the date at the top will say when. For important changes, we will also say so in the notes of the new version on Google Play.
